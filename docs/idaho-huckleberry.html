Idaho huckleberries, briefly

The huckleberry is Idaho's state fruit. It grows wild in mountain forests and ripens in mid to late summer, and it's picked by hand rather than farmed at scale, so it turns up in shakes, pies, pancakes, sauces and cocktails, especially in north Idaho and the resort towns. Menus with huckleberry change with the season, so check before you go.

Huckleberry dishes these menus name most: huckleberry cheeseburger (2), huckleberry lemonade (2), huckleberry shake (2), 511 real huckleberry shake (1), apple huckleberry pie (october special) (1), beast burger with huckleberry jalapeño bacon jam (1).

Huckleberry questions

When is huckleberry season in Idaho?

Wild huckleberries ripen in mid to late summer, later at higher elevations. Some places serve huckleberry dishes only then or on a seasonal menu; each place's note says what its 2025 or 2026 menu named.

Is the huckleberry Idaho's state fruit?

Yes. The Idaho Legislature named the huckleberry the state fruit in 2000.
