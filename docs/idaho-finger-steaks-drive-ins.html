Idaho finger steaks & drive-ins

Finger steaks, briefly

Finger steaks are strips of steak, battered and deep-fried, served with fries and a dipping sauce, often fry sauce (ketchup mixed with mayonnaise). They've been on Boise menus since the 1950s and are still a fixture at Idaho drive-ins, alongside burgers, shakes and fry sauce.

Steaks, drive-ins and fry sauce, counted

18 of the 21 serve finger steaks, seven are drive-ins (a place can be both), and five list fry sauce on the menu.

Dishes these menus name most: finger steaks (15), fry sauce (5), burgers (4), milkshakes (3), tater tots (3), boston shake (2).

How this list was made: for every place we read a 2025 or 2026 source, usually its own menu page or a dated story in Idaho news, and matched the street address to it. A place whose only source was older, whose website had gone dark, or whose menu named the dish just in passing stayed off. Review sites played no part, and the places run by region and town, not by anybody's score. Menus change, so check before you go. Our sources are on the about Idaho Eats page.

Finger steak questions

What are finger steaks?

Strips of steak, battered and deep-fried like chicken strips, served with fries and a dipping sauce. They're an Idaho specialty you'll mostly find at drive-ins, diners and taverns.

What is fry sauce?

A pink sauce of ketchup and mayonnaise, sometimes with pickle juice or spices, for dipping fries and finger steaks. Five of the 21 places here list it.

What counts as a drive-in here?

A burger stand with a walk-up window, carhop or drive-thru service, as the place itself or local news describes it. Each one was checked by hand against a 2025 or 2026 source.
